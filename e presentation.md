Eco design regulations are mandatory legal frameworks that require manufacturers to reduce the environmental impact of their products throughout their entire life cycle. They push companies to design goods with better energy efficiency, durability, and recyclability while advancing a circular economy.

Core Requirements

These regulations typically enforce strict criteria at the product design stage, including: Energy Efficiency: Minimizing power consumption during both active use and standby modes. Material Efficiency: Using recycled content and eliminating hazardous substances that ~~hinder~~ recyclability. Circular Design: Improving product durability, reusability, upgradability, and repairability. Product Passports: Implementing digital tools to track a product's supply chain, material composition, and environmental footprint. Major Frameworks While the concept is globally recognized and often follows standards like ISO 14006, the most ~~prominent~~ framework is the European Union's Eco-design for Sustainable Products Regulation (ESPR). The ESPR replaced the older Eco-design Directive, broadening its reach from strictly energy-related goods (like fridges and lightbulbs) to almost all physical goods sold in the EU. Business Impact If a product does not meet the specified minimum ~~ecological~~ and performance standards, it cannot be placed on the market. Additionally, policies (such as recent EU measures under the ESPR) directly target waste reduction by banning the destruction of unsold apparel and footwear.
